People searching for a **Speechify free alternative** usually want one of two things: a reader that costs nothing for everyday use, or a lower-cost way to turn study and work documents into audio. The right choice depends less on a long feature list and more on where the text lives, which files you use, and whether you need downloadable audio.

> A practical Speechify free alternative is Microsoft Edge Read Aloud for web pages and text-based PDFs, Apple Speak Screen for text visible on an iPhone or iPad, or Invocly for converting PDF, DOCX, and TXT files into audio. Test your actual document and check current usage limits before choosing.

## What should a Speechify free alternative let you do?

Start with the task you need to complete. A browser reader, an accessibility feature, and a document-to-audio converter all use text-to-speech, but they solve different problems.

Compare these points:

- **Input:** Does it read a web page, visible screen text, PDF, DOCX, or TXT file?
- **Output:** Do you only need live playback, or do you need an MP3 to use later?
- **Limits:** Is the tool built into your device, capped by file size, or limited by the number of conversions?
- **Controls:** Can you choose a voice, change playback speed, pause, and resume comfortably?
- **Document quality:** Can it follow paragraphs in the correct order, and what happens with tables or multiple columns?
- **Privacy:** If you upload a file, does the provider clearly explain how it processes and stores that content?

“Free” should describe a usable workflow, not just the presence of a sign-up button. Plan details can change, so verify current limits on each provider's official site rather than relying on an old comparison table.

## When is Microsoft Edge Read Aloud the best free option?

[Microsoft Edge Read Aloud](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/features/read-aloud) is a strong starting point when your material is already on a web page or in a text-based PDF. It is built into the browser, provides voice and speed controls, and does not require you to upload the document to a separate conversion service.

The trade-off is output. Edge is designed to read content aloud in the browser, not to build a downloadable audio library. Reading order can also become awkward when a PDF uses multiple columns, complex tables, repeated headers, or footnotes. Open a representative file and listen to the first few pages before using it for a long study session.

## When should you use Apple Speak Screen?

[Apple Speak Screen](https://support.apple.com/en-sg/guide/iphone/iph96b214f0/ios) is useful when the text is already visible in a compatible iPhone or iPad app. After enabling Speak Screen in Accessibility settings, a two-finger swipe from the top of the display starts reading supported on-screen text.

This is convenient for short articles, messages, and documents that render cleanly on the device. It is less suitable when you want an MP3, need to process a folder of files, or use an app that does not expose its text properly to accessibility features.

## How does Invocly compare for PDF, DOCX, and TXT files?

Invocly is aimed at a narrower job: turning documents into audio. You can [convert PDF, DOCX, and TXT files into natural-sounding speech](https://invocly.com/), listen online, and download the generated MP3.

The current free plan includes three document conversions with no credit card required and a 5 MB limit per file. Those limits make it suitable for testing the workflow or converting occasional notes and reports. If you have a larger weekly workload, check the current limits shown in Invocly before uploading a long queue.

Invocly works with text that can be extracted from the file. An image-only scanned PDF does not contain a usable text layer, so it may need optical character recognition first. The guide on [practical ways to listen to a PDF](https://invocly.com/blog/how-to-listen-to-a-pdf) explains how to identify that problem before conversion.

## Which Speechify free alternative fits your situation?

Choose based on the material, not the brand name:

- Use **Microsoft Edge** when you mainly read web pages and text-based PDFs and only need live playback.
- Use **Apple Speak Screen** when the material is already visible in a compatible iPhone or iPad app.
- Use **Invocly** when you work with PDF, DOCX, or TXT files and want downloadable MP3 audio.

None of these choices is automatically best for every document. Try the same sample file in the options available to you. Listen for reading order, pronunciation, distracting headers, and whether the controls fit the way you study, commute, or work.

## FAQ

### Is there a completely free alternative to Speechify?
Microsoft Edge Read Aloud and Apple Speak Screen are built into supported devices and do not require a separate text-to-speech subscription. They work best for content already open in the browser or on the screen. A dedicated document converter may be more practical when you need an MP3 file.

### What is a good Speechify free alternative for PDFs?
Microsoft Edge can read many text-based PDFs aloud for free. Invocly is another option when you want to upload a PDF and generate audio that you can listen to online or download as an MP3. Image-only scanned PDFs may need OCR before either workflow can read the text correctly.

### Can Microsoft Edge read a PDF aloud for free?
Yes. Open a text-based PDF in Microsoft Edge and use Read Aloud from the browser controls or context menu. Preview the reading order first because complex columns, tables, headers, and footers can interrupt the narration.

### Can an iPhone read documents aloud without another app?
Apple includes Spoken Content accessibility controls on iPhone and iPad. After enabling Speak Screen, you can swipe down with two fingers to hear supported on-screen text. The result depends on whether the app exposes the document text correctly.

### Can I use Invocly for free document-to-speech conversion?
Invocly currently offers three free document conversions with no credit card required and a 5 MB limit per file. It supports PDF, DOCX, and TXT files and lets you listen online or download the generated MP3. Check the pricing page for current limits before relying on it for a large workload.

### How should I compare free text-to-speech tools?
Compare the file formats you use, whether you need downloadable audio, the free usage limit, voice and speed controls, and how the service handles uploaded files. Test one representative document before committing to a workflow, especially if it contains tables, multiple columns, or scanned pages.
